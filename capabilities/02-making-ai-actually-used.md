# Making AI tools actually get used

A good tool and mandatory training don't guarantee adoption. I've seen this from both sides: rolling out an AI platform to teams who didn't ask for it, and later running discovery with companies trying to do the same thing.

At Le Wagon, over two years, I led the migration of operations teams (bootcamps running in two languages, multiple concurrent programs) onto Dust as the new company-wide AI platform, and supervised the change management for those teams during the transition, in the middle of a reorganization.

Rollout and training got everyone technically able to use the tool. Adoption didn't follow automatically. What moved it:

- Identifying the specific use cases that mattered to each team instead of pushing a generic tool.
- Running a hackathon for the operations teams to surface use cases from the people who'd actually use them, instead of defining them top-down.
- Building a knowledge base so people could find and reuse what already worked instead of solving the same problem twice.
- Breaking down the silos between teams, so a use case solved once didn't need re-solving elsewhere.

The pattern: adoption tracks how well a use case matches an existing habit, not how much training was delivered.
