# Maëlle Waché

**AI transformation · adoption · operations**

I help teams turn business priorities into useful tools and ways of working. I move between leaders, operational teams and technical partners: understanding where work gets stuck, deciding what is worth solving, building or coordinating a practical solution, and staying with people as they learn to use it.

My work draws on operational AI enablement at Le Wagon, transformation diagnosis and prioritisation at Mantu, and go-to-market and product discovery at Raison. Some examples below describe work I have done; situations marked **illustrative** show how the approach can apply elsewhere, not a client engagement or a claimed result.

## Where I can help

| When a team says… | How I approach it | Typical output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “We have AI tools, but people aren't using them in their day-to-day work.” | Observe real workflows, listen to different roles, identify friction and coach people around a task they already need to complete. | Role-specific use cases, hands-on coaching and a feedback loop. |
| “Everyone has ideas, but we don't know where to start.” | Interview stakeholders, map needs and dependencies, compare value, feasibility, ownership and effort. | A prioritised use-case portfolio and a staged roadmap. |
| “Our teams are spending too much time on repetitive work.” | Map the current process, locate the checks that need judgment, prototype a lighter workflow and test it with the people doing the work. | A practical assistant or automation with a clear human review step. |
| “Business and technical teams aren't aligned.” | Translate needs into concrete problems and acceptance criteria; bring technical constraints back to the business; iterate with users. | A shared brief, decision points and a testable first version. |
| “We need to learn from customers and act on it.” | Structure feedback by theme, preserve context and trace observations back to source, then separate evidence from interpretation. | A usable synthesis for product, marketing or operations. |
| “We are busy, but priorities and follow-through are scattered.” | Organise objectives, actions, decisions and signals into a simple operating rhythm, with human validation where needed. | A daily view, weekly priorities and a clearer decision log. |

## From strategy to daily use

### 1. Understand before choosing a solution

I interview stakeholders and spend time with teams to understand what actually happens: where handoffs break, what takes time, what decisions are difficult and what people already do to work around a problem. I look at the organisation, the process and the people as well as the tools.

**Example — transformation diagnosis, Mantu.** I conducted stakeholder interviews, analysed recurring needs and helped prioritise initiatives across a broader transformation portfolio. The work led to a structured diagnosis and recommendations for a roadmap. The scope here was diagnosis and prioritisation; it should not be read as a claim that every recommendation was deployed.

### 2. Prioritise and turn insight into a plan

I make trade-offs visible: business value, effort, dependencies, data readiness, risk and who can own the outcome. A useful roadmap connects near-term experiments to longer-term change and defines what each sprint should teach us.

**Illustrative example:** a company has ten proposed AI use cases. Before selecting the most impressive one, I would check whether a team owns the workflow, whether the data is usable and whether a small first test can produce useful feedback. [Read the composite case](case-studies/01-prioritizing-the-first-use-case.md).

### 3. Build around the work

At Le Wagon I designed assistants and automations for operational tasks using tools such as Dust, Make and Zapier. Examples include helping review invoices, synthesising learner feedback and supporting coordination. The aim was to reduce friction in a real process while keeping accountable people in control.

[Invoice review case](case-studies/03-automating-invoice-compliance-checks.md) · [AI platform adoption case](case-studies/02-migrating-a-team-to-a-new-ai-platform.md)

### 4. Help people make it their own

I have organised hackathons and practical team sessions, created moments for people to share what they have tried, and supported colleagues one-to-one. I adapt the starting point to the person's role, confidence and workflow: a useful first step for one person may be a simple prompt; for another, it may be an assistant integrated into an existing process.

Adoption is not a one-off training event. I look for whether the solution fits the work, whether people know when to trust or question it, and who can maintain it after launch.

### 5. Listen, measure and adapt

I use qualitative feedback and appropriate operational indicators together. Access to a tool, completion of training and meaningful use are different signals. Estimates need a defined scope, and a useful metric must help someone make a better decision.

## Systems and tools I have built

I have used Claude, APIs and MCP connectors to build internal tools that address operational needs. These descriptions focus on their purpose; the underlying data, configurations and company-specific workflows remain private.

| Tool type | Problem it helps address | My contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Voice of customer cockpit** | Feedback is scattered across conversations and difficult to reuse. | Built a way to organise observations into themes for teams to explore and act on. |
| **Outbound analytics** | Outreach activity and replies are difficult to read as a coherent picture. | Built an analysis view to help understand activity and responses. |
| **Event tracking** | Relevant opportunities and events are dispersed. | Built a way to collect and organise event information. |
| **GTM and daily work cockpit** | Priorities, actions and decisions can get lost across tools. | Built views and routines to make operational follow-through easier. |

These are internal tools; this repository does not publish their code or private data. A working system also needs source permissions, reliable data and explicit ownership. I use AI to prepare information and proposals, with human review for decisions and external actions.

## Examples by team

The following are **illustrative applications**, not claims of projects completed in each function.

- **Operations:** turn repetitive checks, handoffs or reporting into a simpler workflow with a review step.
- **Customer-facing teams:** turn notes and recurring questions into structured insight while preserving source context.
- **Marketing and sales:** understand outreach activity, spot patterns in feedback and prepare better-informed next steps.
- **Learning and people teams:** run practical workshops based on employees' tasks, then support first use and peer sharing.
- **Transformation leaders:** connect interviews, dependencies and opportunities to an accountable roadmap.
- **Product and technology:** translate business needs into testable requirements and bring user feedback back into iteration.
