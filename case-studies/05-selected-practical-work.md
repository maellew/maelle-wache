# From transformation to everyday work

A selection of real work across AI adoption, operations and transformation. These short cases describe my role and approach at a level suitable for a public portfolio. They omit internal data, client details and unverified performance figures.

## 1. Turning learner feedback into operational insight

**Context.** Program teams receive qualitative feedback during a learning journey. Reading and structuring responses takes time, while some issues need prompt human attention.

**My contribution.** At Le Wagon, I worked on an AI assistant that turns survey responses into a structured summary and helps highlight situations for the team to review. I started from the team's existing process, configured the assistant around its needs and supported its use.

**How it works.** Responses → proposed themes and points to follow up → review by the team → appropriate next step. The assistant prepares information; people decide what to do and review any communication.

**What this illustrates.** Using AI to make qualitative feedback easier to act on while keeping context and human judgment in the loop. Specific time savings are not included here because their scope needs verification.

## 2. Supporting invoice review with human judgment

**Context.** Operational teams needed to check invoices against rules that varied by context. The work was repetitive but required care and a responsible reviewer.

**My contribution.** I designed an assistant to support the checking workflow and flag points that need a person's attention. I worked from the team's process and supported the team in using the tool.

**What this illustrates.** A practical automation that assists a consequential review instead of making an autonomous legal or tax decision. [Read the case](03-automating-invoice-compliance-checks.md).

## 3. Helping teams adopt AI in their own work

**Context.** Making a tool available and explaining its features does not automatically make it part of someone's routine. Different people have different roles, levels of confidence and constraints.

**My contribution.** At Le Wagon, I identified use cases with operations colleagues, organised hands-on hackathons and sharing sessions, and supported individuals one-to-one. We worked on tasks participants already knew, then used their feedback to improve the experience.

**How I approached it.** Start with a real task → try a first version together → share and document useful approaches → support people as they adapt them to their work.

**What this illustrates.** Adoption as a combination of relevant use cases, practical learning, peer support and ongoing ownership. [Read more](02-migrating-a-team-to-a-new-ai-platform.md).

## 4. Giving a cross-functional transformation a clearer direction

**Context.** Transformation programmes can involve many initiatives and stakeholders, each with their own priorities and dependencies. A shared view is needed before decisions can be sequenced.

**My contribution.** During my work at Mantu, I interviewed stakeholders, analysed needs and friction, and contributed to a transformation diagnosis and prioritisation recommendations. I brought the perspectives of different teams together to make dependencies and decisions easier to see.

**Output.** A structured diagnosis and proposed priorities and next steps. The roadmap and actions were recommendations; this case does not claim that every proposal was deployed or produced a measured impact.

**What this illustrates.** Moving between strategic direction and operational reality, and making a complex portfolio easier to discuss and prioritise.

## 5. Bringing field insight back into the business

**Context.** Organisations can have access to AI tools yet still face challenges embedding useful practices in day-to-day work. Understanding those challenges requires listening across roles and organisations.

**My contribution.** At Raison, where I currently work as Founding GTM & Operations Lead, I conduct customer discovery and contribute to go-to-market and operations alongside the team. I help structure recurring needs and bring field feedback into conversations about positioning, the offer and product priorities.

**What this illustrates.** Connecting conversations in the field with the decisions made by a team building a product. This is work in my current employed role; client identities, commercial details and internal materials are not shared here.

## 6. Building tools for insight and everyday coordination

**Context.** Feedback, outreach information and work priorities can be scattered across tools, making it harder to see patterns or decide what needs attention.

**My contribution.** I have built internal tools with AI and connected technologies to help organise qualitative feedback, review outreach activity and support daily work. I start with a concrete workflow, build a first version and improve it with user feedback.

**What this illustrates.** Combining operational understanding and hands-on building. The tools' code, configurations, source data and organisation-specific processes remain private; availability or results should not be inferred from this summary.
