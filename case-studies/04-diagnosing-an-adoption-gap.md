# Diagnosing an AI adoption gap and building a 90-day plan

*Case exercise, built for an assessment process, using a fictional company and fictional data.*

**Context**: leadership believed an AI platform rollout was working: most staff reported using it regularly. The usage data told a different story: only a small share of sessions reflected complete, real workflows, and average session length was a few minutes. Training had happened, licenses were deployed, but the platform hadn't actually changed how people worked.

**What I did**: started from the gap between the story leadership was telling and what the data showed, then split it into three separate causes instead of one: declared use without real workflow completion, a champion programme that existed on paper with no budget or protected time, and a mismatch between the platform's early positioning and where real expertise already sat in the organization. Each needed a different fix. I mapped stakeholders by what each one actually needed rather than by title, and proposed testing the riskiest assumptions (why some offices lagged, whether any sensitive data had already leaked into unapproved tools, whether the people meant to champion the rollout were still available) in week one, before committing to the rest of a 90-day plan.

**Result**: a phased plan built around behavioral metrics (real workflow completion, sentiment, habit change at 30/60/90 days) instead of access metrics, with one measurable commitment per stakeholder. The point: an organization can hit 100% licenses deployed and 0% actual change, and those two numbers look identical on a slide until you separate access from behavior.
