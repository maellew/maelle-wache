# Prioritizing the first AI use case in a B2B services company

*Composite case, rebuilt from several similar situations. The company, the numbers, and the people are not real.*

**Context**: a B2B services company, a few hundred people, wants to "do AI" but hasn't picked a use case. Leadership has a list of about ten ideas, one per department.

**Starting assumption**: start with the use case with the biggest visible upside, the one that makes the best story in a leadership meeting.

**What changed my mind**: that high-potential use case touched a process with many stakeholders and no clear owner on the business side. Nobody had the mandate to decide the details. The smaller use cases, owned by a single team with a manager who could decide alone, moved three times faster in early conversations.

**What I didn't do, and why**: build a detailed business case to get leadership to pick between the ten ideas. Skipped because it would have delayed the first real learning by months, for a decision that was going to shift anyway once something got deployed.

**What happened instead**: prioritized the smallest use case with a real owner and a real decision scope, even though it looked less impressive on paper.

**Result**: deployment went from idea to real usage in a few weeks instead of months of back and forth. What we learned from that first case fed directly into prioritizing the next ones.
